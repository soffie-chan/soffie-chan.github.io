OVERVIEW
Inspired by Manfred Ludwig's 'Viva Topo!', our goal was to create a board game within three weeks utilizing elements of its original game. We used Google Slides to create our game board and playtested using this tool, as well. Since we were limited to a digital space, I utilized Procreate to design assets and Google Docs to write the rulebook.

The outcome was The King's Chosen, a medieval-fantasy themed game, where players play as army generals and work to keep their armies alive and successful as they fight a dragon and assist villagers around the kingdom.


ROLE
As the game designer, I had full control over the design, rules, and playtesting of the game. I designed assets, wrote and compiled the rules in a rulebook, conducted playtesting sessions, and iterated the design based on the playtest feedback.

DESIGN PROCESS

  • Interpreting the prompt:
     I utilized theme-driven design before implementing mechanic-driven design, as I had already set my mind on creating a medieval-fantasy themed game. I imagined the 'Viva Topo!' world and characters first within the theme's setting, then developed the mechanics to support the theme. I wanted to ensure that players understood the rules and mechanics despite the aesthetic, so I tested my rules on the initial Viva Topo! board before creating The King's Chosen board.


  • Inspirations:
    I enjoy playing fantastical adventure games like Skyrim and Baldur's Gate 3. Having much to do and mythical elements such as enchanted weapons and otherworldly creatures piques my interest often, so it felt the most exciting to pursue. Moreover, when I played "Viva Topo!", I felt as though there should be a way to attack the pursuer and use the environment to my advantage, so the idea of slaying the dragon and helping villagers for advantages fell into place naturally. Conducting playtests with players that don't often play medieval-fantasy games and watching them enjoy it only made me more enthusiastic about the idea.


  • Design Challenges:
    One of the biggest challenges observed during playtest 1 was figuring out how to include an environmental aspect (i.e, surrounding villages) without making the game complicated or overwhelmingly long. During my first rules draft, players enjoyed the idea of slaying the dragon so much that they did not utilize the villages for shelter. Therefore, I introduced the concept of "village quests" (side objectives to earn points and advantages against the dragon) to urge players to visit the villages in the board corners. This worked very well, as observed in my second playtest!
    Other challenges related to edge-cases in the rulebook, which were eventually clarified further.

PLAYTESTING

I conducted playtesting sessions with various participants to observe how they interpreted the rules, how they interacted with the game, the emotions they felt, and the challenges or successes they encountered. Each play session involved either two completely new players or one new player and myself, the developer.
